# Bogotá 1995 — v0

The first playable room. One question: **when you interact with this little room, do you feel the thing you're imagining?**

Everything below was decided in the alignment interview (September 2026). `vision.md` holds the long-term ideas; this file holds only what v0 is.

## The moment

- **Where:** a teenager's bedroom (15–17 years old) in a red-brick apartment in Chapinero / Teusaquillo, facing the cerros.
- **When:** Saturday 28 October 1995, starting around 5:30 p.m. It's raining (rainy season). Aterciopelados' *El Dorado* came out four days earlier.
- **Clock:** real time. Dusk falls while you're there. At 6:00 p.m. every station and channel plays the national anthem.
- Every visit starts at the same moment. Only the tape you recorded (and a few small flags) carry over.

## Design principles

1. **Behaviors with friction, not media on demand.** The media is the reward for patience, timing and luck. Calibrate the friction: long enough to recognize the impatience, short enough not to be a chore.
2. **The world runs without you.** Radio and TV are broadcasts on a clock. You tune into whatever is on now.
3. **You call a place, not a person.** Households, gatekeepers, messages that travel slowly.
4. **Specific over generic.** Bogotá, 1995, in Bogotá Spanish. No generic "90s aesthetic".
5. **No museum labels.** No tutorials, no explanations, no goals on screen.
6. **Fidelity first.** Fictional people; real events only when true. (Deviations belong to the later Lost Futures idea.)

## What's in v0

| Object | What you can do |
|---|---|
| **Grabadora** (radio + cassette) — the centerpiece | Tune through static into live stations. Record whatever is playing onto a blank tape (the DJ over the intro, the static, the ad). Rewind in (scaled) real time with the counter spinning. Play back your imperfect copy. The tape persists. |
| **Rotary phone + libreta** | Dial from a handwritten phone book. A friend's mother answers ("¿De parte de quién?"), lines are busy, numbers don't exist, the grandmother won't let you go, someone's on the other extension. The phone rings with no caller ID. |
| **TV** (small color set with knobs) | CRT on/off, channel knob where most positions are snow, antenna you drag to fix the picture, a channel of real 1995 clips (once approved). |
| **Window** | Rain, the cerros and Monserrate in and out of the clouds, busetas, the street at dusk. Open it, wipe the fog. |

**One designed thread, never announced:** the DJ reads the request-line number → you call (busy, redial) → you request a song and a dedication → you wait → the DJ plays it, talking over the intro → you race to REC → you rewind and hear your copy.

## Not in v0

PC / Windows / Encarta · camera · backpack · Walkman · VHS · CDs · leaving the room · any runtime AI (no generated dialogue, no agents) · Lost Futures deviations · archive research and licensing · quests, scores, tutorials · neon / Memphis / VHS filters · backend, accounts, analytics · 3D, VR · English · polished mobile.

**Paying SAYCO-ACINPRO like a bar: first thoughts, to confirm with them.**
A bar's license (from OSA, the joint SAYCO-ACINPRO office) covers music
played inside the venue. A website is a different use: it streams to people
wherever they are, and the recordings themselves belong to the labels and
performers. The closest model is an internet-radio license, but two things
the room does look more like on-demand listening: the request line (you pick
the song) and the cassette (you keep a copy). On-demand use usually needs the
labels' permission, not only the collecting societies'. If we go that way,
the questions to ask:

1. Is there a web-radio tariff for a small non-commercial site, and does it
   cover both the authors (SAYCO) and the performers and producers (ACINPRO)?
2. Does letting a listener request a song, or record it, change the category?
3. Who licenses hosting the recordings themselves (the labels)?
4. Does it cover listeners outside Colombia?

**Known limits:**

  be on a real GPU.

  web server (YouTube refuses `file://`). They don't play in the claude.ai
  link either (artifacts can't embed other sites).

## Fact-check list

Real-world claims used in the content. ✅ = checked against a source or your
memory, ❓ = needs your memory or a better source.

- ✅ Aterciopelados' *El Dorado* released 24 Oct 1995 (singles «Bolero falaz», «Florecita rockera»).
- ✅ First Rock al Parque: 26–29 May 1995, more than 80,000 people.
- ✅ Colombia's call-progress tones are 425 Hz (ringback 1 s on / 4.5 s off, busy 0.25/0.25). From a 2003 ITU bulletin; assumed the same in 1995.
- ✅ Bogotá home numbers had 7 digits (your memory).
- ✅ 117 gave the time (ETB), with a mature woman's recorded voice until 1999.
- ✅ Law 198 of July 1995 made the anthem mandatory on radio and TV at 6 a.m. and 6 p.m.

- ✅ Mockus's "hora zanahoria" (bars close at 1 a.m.) started in 1995; mimes and citizen-culture campaigns in 1995.
