# Bogotá 1995 — Interactive Nostalgia / Lost Futures

## Where the idea started

The initial idea was to create some kind of artifact — website, game, video, interactive experience, or something else — around the nostalgia of someone living in **Bogotá during the 1990s**.

Early memory triggers included:

- Aterciopelados
- OkiDoki
- cassettes and CDs
- television
- radio
- old devices
- Bogotá itself

The idea quickly became more specific and interesting than a generic “90s nostalgia” project.

The current best anchor is roughly **Bogotá, 1995**.

That period gives us an interesting overlap of cassette and CD culture, Colombian rock, 90s television, pre-Internet everyday life, Bogotá street culture, school/adolescence, landlines, VHS, early PCs, etc.

---

# Core idea

Create an **interactive memory space** representing a Bogotá bedroom/living room around 1995.

Rather than presenting a museum with labels explaining old objects, the visitor should **perform behaviors that have disappeared**.

Examples:

- rewind a cassette
- tune a radio
- wait for a song to begin
- try to record it without the DJ speaking over the intro
- dial a seven-digit telephone number
- call a friend's house and have their mother answer
- change channels on a CRT television
- insert a VHS
- open a CD case and inspect the booklet
- use an old PC
- perhaps browse Encarta
- take a photograph without immediately seeing it
- look through a backpack or handwritten notebook

The central design principle is:

> Nostalgia should come from recovering lost behaviors, not merely seeing old objects.

---

# Why a room?

The first mental image was a room containing objects that act as **portals into memories**.

For example:

### Cassette deck / radio

Tune stations, hear static, record something, rewind tapes, discover mixtapes, hear fragments of the era.

### Television

Channel surfing between Colombian television, commercials, news, cartoons, programs, static, etc.

### Telephone

Dial someone. Hear ringing. Someone else may answer. No caller ID, messaging or smartphone.

### Window

Rainy Bogotá, gray light, red brick, buses, street sounds, distant mountains, neighborhood life.

### PC

Old operating system, Encarta-like interactions, files, games, perhaps eventually early dial-up Internet.

### Backpack

School notebooks, handwritten messages, stickers, Walkman, football cards, homework, etc.

### Camera / photo roll

A finite number of photographs and no immediate preview.

Each object could briefly take the visitor somewhere else:

**room → memory → return to room**

Eventually this could grow:

**room → apartment → building → neighborhood → Bogotá**

But that is _not_ the first version.

---

# 2D/2.5D versus 3D

The initial thought was a 3D environment.

After discussing it, the preferred first approach became **2D or 2.5D point-and-click**.

Think illustrated interactive room rather than free-roaming videogame.

Advantages:

- dramatically easier to build
- greater artistic control
- easier to create a cohesive nostalgic aesthetic
- easier to fill the space with historically specific details
- avoids spending months creating 3D assets
- more appropriate for an experimental side project

Full 3D remains a possible later evolution.

---

# Reference we found: Rewind Room

A particularly important reference is **Rewind Room**, an interactive 1998 bedroom.

It independently uses almost exactly the interaction model we had imagined:

- television
- stereo
- landline
- VHS
- CDs
- pager
- computer
- game console
- clickable objects
- small discoveries and interactions

Juan visited it and **loved it**.

This is useful confirmation that the basic experience can be delightful.

However:

**Do not make Bogotá 1995 merely a Colombian reskin of Rewind Room.**

The distinctive value should come from Bogotá, Colombian culture, specific memories, lost behaviors, historical atmosphere, and eventually the deeper conceptual layers discussed below.

Other references mentioned:

- **Rooms.xyz** — example of interactive web-based 3D rooms/worlds.
- **Infinite Mac** — shows how historically accurate old computing experiences can actually run interactively in a browser.

# Lost behaviors

This emerged as possibly the emotional center of the project.

Life had **friction, scarcity and waiting**.

You waited for:

- the song on the radio
- photographs to be developed
- a television show to air
- someone to call
- someone to arrive
- a CD to become available
- a cassette to rewind
- a computer to start
- information to reach you

You could leave home and become **temporarily unreachable**.

Information arrived through objects and places rather than being omnipresent.

This may ultimately be more powerful than the surface-level nostalgia.

# Historical media and feasibility

Technically, the experience is very feasible.

The difficult issue is not implementing the room.

The difficult issue is **media rights**.

Potential research/archive sources discussed:

- Señal Memoria / RTVC
- Archivo de Bogotá
- publicly available historical photographs
- historical Colombian advertising
- old television/radio material
- official or permitted online embeds

Actual recordings by Aterciopelados, OkiDoki footage, TV broadcasts, etc. need to be treated carefully from a copyright/licensing perspective.

But much of the environment can be original:

- Bogotá rain
- cassette mechanics
- radio static
- dialing
- ringing phones
- CRT noises
- bus ambience
- room ambience
- modem sounds
- original illustrations
- fictional period-appropriate media
- recreated interactions

Therefore copyright should **not block the prototype**.

Placeholder media is acceptable initially.

# Important technical idea: stateful objects

A later realization was that objects should ideally not just contain click handlers.

They could have actual **state and actions**.

Example:

cassette_deck

state:

- tape
- door open/closed
- current position
- playing/stopped/rewinding
- record state

actions:

- open
- close
- eject
- insert
- play
- stop
- rewind
- fast_forward
- record

A human clicks PLAY.

An AI agent could call exactly the same `play()` action.

Therefore the visible room could eventually be only one interface into an underlying **simulated world**.

This architectural choice could be useful even if agents come much later.

# Possible agent environment

Later, the environment could become navigable by an AI agent.

Unlike modern agents, it would have no instant Internet/search/API omniscience.

It would need to operate using the information structures of the period:

- telephone
- radio
- television
- newspapers
- books
- encyclopedias
- notes
- other people
- physical locations
- limited memory/resources
- waiting

Possible tasks:

> It is Bogotá, Saturday, June 1995. You promised to meet Andrés this afternoon but don't remember where. Figure it out.

The agent might:

- find an address book
- locate Andrés's number
- use the landline
- speak with his mother
- discover he's not home
- search the backpack
- find a note
- determine what bus it needs

Another:

> Record “Florecita Rockera” onto this cassette without cutting off the beginning.

This requires understanding:

- radio
- cassette recording
- timing
- physical state

Another:

> You're bored at home on a rainy Sunday in Bogotá in 1995. Find something to do.

This begins to make the world interesting as an **agent environment**, not merely a nostalgic interface.

# Mark Fisher / Lost Futures

Juan immediately connected the idea with **Mark Fisher's “lost futures.”**

This produced the most conceptually ambitious possible direction.

Instead of merely asking:

> Can we reconstruct Bogotá in 1995?

the project could eventually ask:

> What futures were still imaginable from 1995 that never became our present?

The agent could initially inhabit this reconstructed world **without knowing 2026**.

Its knowledge and possibilities would be constrained by the environment itself.

It should _not_ simply be an LLM instructed:

> Pretend it is 1995.

Instead, the world determines what information and actions are available.

Eventually, alternative history could emerge.

Questions include:

- What if the Web evolved around small personal communities rather than giant centralized platforms?
- What if physical/local music culture persisted differently?
- What if Colombian/Bogotá cultural and civic developments took another path?
- What technologies might emerge if an intelligent agent began from assumptions shaped by cassettes, landlines, broadcast media and encyclopedias rather than smartphones, APIs and search engines?

The reconstructed past could gradually become a **past from which another future emerges**.

Potential subtle narrative device:

At first everything seems historically accurate.

Then small deviations appear.

- A newspaper reports something that never happened.
- A plausible technology appears that did not exist in our timeline.
- A musician's career evolves differently.
- The agent leaves notes about something it is building.
- The visitor slowly realizes this is no longer our past.

Eventually:

> Whose nostalgia is this, if these memories never actually happened?

This could turn the project into something considerably stranger and richer than a nostalgia website.

But:

**THIS IS A LATER-STAGE IDEA.**

Do not burden the prototype with it.

Keep “Lost Futures / agents / alternate history” in a separate conceptual notebook.

# The first real research question

Not:

> Can this become a successful game/platform/agent benchmark?

Not:

> How do we build a virtual Bogotá?

Not:

> How do we commercialize this?

Instead:

> **When I interact with this little room, do I feel the thing I'm imagining?**

Ideally someone who experienced Bogotá in the 1990s should click something and unexpectedly think:

> Oh wow. I remember this.

If the room produces that emotional response, there is evidence worth continuing.

If it doesn't, we learn cheaply.
